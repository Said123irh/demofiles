## PROJECT SUMMARY
- **Series:** Audience-choice fantasy adventure, D&D inspired, original world (no official D&D IP).
- **Channel language:** English narration.
- **Format:** Long form YouTube videos. Pixel art animation (16x16 or 32x32 style sprites), about 4 minutes per standard episode, one episode per day. Follow PART 4 (Animation Cost Saving Guide) so episodes stay cheap to animate.
- **Core mechanic:** Every episode ends with exactly 2 choices on screen. Audience votes (YouTube Community poll plus pinned comment, reply A or B). The winner is animated next.
- **No dice, no meters, no stats.** Keep it simple for new viewers.
- **Every episode starts with a 10 second recap** ("Last time...") and ends with 2 choices.
- **Introduce only 1 new character per episode**, with a one line tag.
- **Story spine stays fixed.** Audience steers the middle, and all choices must lead back to the spine.
- **Writing style:** simple plain English, no em dashes, kid friendly language.
- **Hero:** Wick Marrow, 17, Lamplighter.
- **Episode 1 script:** see ashenmoor-episode-1.md.

## 1. THE WORLD: ASHENMOOR

A hundred years ago, the kingdom of **Eldmere** was bright and rich. In its capital stood the **Sunwell**, a tower holding a captured piece of the sun. Its light kept the monsters of the **Underlight** (the dark world below) locked away.

One night the Sunwell went dark. No one knows why. Eldmere fell. Now the land is called **Ashenmoor**, a place of long twilight, grey fog and hungry things.

People survive in small villages protected by **lamps**. As long as a lamp burns, monsters stay away. People who keep these lamps lit are called **Lamplighters**. Every year there are fewer of them and the lamps are failing.

### The Five Old Powers (gods)
| God | Domain | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Aurel, the Sun-Mother** | Light, life | Silent since the Sunwell fell |
| **Korrun, the Tide-Father** | Sea, storms | Sailors still pray to him |
| **Dwenna, the Hearth-Smith** | Fire, craft, home | Dwarves follow her |
| **Vix, the Trickster** | Luck, lies, change | Goblins and thieves love her |
| **The Pale Warden** | Death, rest | Not evil. Just patient. |

### Places (our map for the whole series)
1. **Emberfall** (start): small village at the edge of the forest
2. **The Dimwood**: cursed forest, glowing mushrooms, Gloomhounds
3. **Saltgrave Harbor**: pirate port built on a ship graveyard
4. **Ironhollow**: dwarf mines, now sealed from inside
5. **Mirror Fen**: swamp where reflections lie and a hag trades secrets
6. **Cinder Peak**: sleeping dragon, **Cinderwyrm**
7. **The Hollow Court**: the villain's dead palace
8. **The Sunwell Tower**: the end

### Monsters
- **Gloomhounds**: wolf shapes made of shadow, afraid of light
- **Wick-Moths**: giant moths that eat flames
- **Hollowfolk**: empty armor and clothes walking around, no one inside
- **Bone-Wights**: skeleton soldiers of old Eldmere
- **Marsh Hag Oda**: trades secrets for memories
- **Cinderwyrm**: ancient dragon, not evil, just guarding something

## 2. THE HERO

**Name: Wick Marrow**, age 17
**Class:** Lamplighter (custom class: light magic, no real weapon)
**Look (pixel design):** small, patched grey cloak, messy dark hair, big boots, one tiny glowing lantern on a stick. Easy to draw in 16x16 or 32x32 pixels.

**Personality:** Brave in action, scared in his head. Talks to himself. Cracks bad jokes when nervous. Loyal to a fault.

**Flaw:** Terrified of total darkness, which is a big problem in a world getting darker.

**Secret (we reveal slowly):** His lantern's flame never goes out, even in a storm or underwater. That is because the flame is a piece of the Sunwell, and inside it is the spirit of **Queen Isolde**, the last queen of Eldmere. She cannot speak yet. Later she will.

**Powers:**
- **Glow:** lantern lights up a small area, scares weak monsters
- **Flare:** one big burst of light (tiring, so Wick cannot spam it)
- **Ember Sight:** sees hidden things in the dark (tiring too)

## 3. THE VILLAIN

**The Hollow King, Aldric the Unlit.**
He was King Aldric, Isolde's husband. He was dying of illness and terrified of death. He tried to put his own life into the Sunwell to live forever. It went wrong. The Sunwell went dark, Isolde's spirit was trapped in the last spark, and Aldric became the Hollow King, a king of empty armor who cannot die, cannot feel, and cannot stop.

**What he wants now:** To find the last spark (Wick's lantern) and put out the sun forever so no one ever has to die again, because he thinks that if the world is empty nobody can lose anything.

**Why he is a good villain:** He is wrong, but you understand him.

### The Big Twist and Ending Choice (for ~episode 60+)
Wick can **relight the Sunwell** (the sun returns, but Isolde's spirit is gone) or **free Aldric** (and the world stays twilight). A third path, chosen by the audience, can be a mix. The ending is the biggest poll of the series.

## 5. HOW THE AUDIENCE CHOICE WORKS

### Keep it simple for new viewers
- **No meters or stats on screen.** Risk is shown by the story itself. If a choice sounds dangerous, the narrator says so in one line.
- **10 second recap at the start of every episode:** "Last time: Wick found a strange key under the chapel. You voted to go down. Let's see what's there."
- **Every episode ends with exactly 2 big choices** on screen, one short line each.
- Bad outcomes are always story consequences (Wick gets hurt, an ally is lost, a door locks behind him). Never an instant "game over".

### Types of choices (rotate them so it stays fresh)
- Go to a new location
- Add a new party member (A or B from two candidates)
- Fight or run
- Trust or betray
- Open the door or leave it shut
- Use an item or save it
- Secret pick: one option is hidden and only appears if the comments guess it

## 6. THE POLL SYSTEM (YouTube)

As far as I know, YouTube comments do not have a built in poll. Here is the best setup:

1. **Community tab poll:** post a 2 option poll right when the video goes live. This gives you a real vote count.
2. **Pinned comment backup:** pin a comment saying "Reply A or B". Easy and people love replying.
3. **End of video:** show both choices on screen with big pixel buttons: "A: ... B: ...". Say "Vote in the Community tab or comment A or B."
4. **Timing idea:** upload at the same time daily (for example 6 PM IST). Poll closes about 20 hours later, and the winning choice gets animated for the next day.
5. **Next video opens** with a quick "You chose B!" recap so new viewers get it.
6. Keep a **Canon Tracker** (simple sheet) with: episode, choice offered, winner, vote %, party members, key story facts. This stops mistakes and saves tokens too.

**Production tip:** daily uploads are heavy. Keep each episode **3 to 5 minutes**, and keep 3 episodes in the bank so you never run out.

## 7. WHERE EACH CHOICE LEADS (Episode 2 plan; Episode 1 script is in its own file)

### If A wins: "Down the Stair"
Wick goes alone into the crypt. He finds old Eldmere murals showing the Sunwell and a queen holding a lantern that looks exactly like his. A Bone-Wight guards the inner door. Ends with a choice: fight it, trick it, or run.

### If B wins: "What Bram Hid"
Bram goes pale at the key. He admits he is the last member of a secret order, the Keepers, who guard the crypt. He gives Wick a map and a warning, and tells him the village will not survive the winter without the Sunwell. Ends with a choice: leave tonight, or wait for morning and try to save Emberfall first.

Either way Wick ends up with the same knowledge by episode 4 (his lantern is special), just by a different road.

## 5. MAKING THE WORLD FEEL ALIVE

**Money:** *Embers* (small glowing coins). A bowl of soup costs 2 embers.

**Everyday sayings (use them again and again, fans will repeat them):**
- "Keep your wick trimmed." (be careful)
- "May your lamp never flicker." (good luck)
- "Dark take it!" (swearing, kid friendly)

**Food:** Tilda's rock hard bread, mushroom stew, fen eel pie, dwarven fire pepper soup.

**Festival:** The **Night of Many Lamps** once a year. Great for a special episode where the whole audience's past choices show up as lamps.

**Rumor board:** In every town there is a notice board. Each shows rumors that hint at future choices ("a strange light in the Fen", "ghost ship seen near Saltgrave"). This gives you easy story ideas and makes viewers feel the world goes on without Wick.

**Seasons:** Ashenmoor has Long Dusk (autumn), Frostdark (winter), Greymelt (spring). Summer no longer exists. Bringing summer back could be a lovely reward at the end.

## 6. HOW RECURRING CHARACTERS HELP THE AUDIENCE CHOICE

- Let viewers vote on **who to trust**, not just where to go.
- If a character the audience loves is lost, it hurts. If the audience saves them, it feels earned.
- Remember: **the audience's past votes should come back**. A creature they spared returns later to help. A door they left shut opens at a bad time. Viewers love seeing their choices matter.

# PART 4: ANIMATION COST SAVING GUIDE
Goal: keep 4 minute daily episodes cheap to animate. Cost depends on how many different things get animated, not on running time.

## 4.1 Episode sizes
| Type | Length | Scenes | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard** | about 4 min | 5 to 6 max | Most episodes |
| **Light** | 2 to 3 min | 3 to 4 | Breather days, simple choices, travel |
| **Big** | 5 to 6 min | 7 to 8 | Boss fights and major reveals only (about 1 in 8 episodes) |

## 4.2 The 12 rules
1. **Only animate the winning choice.** Write both outcomes of a poll, but animate only the one the audience picks. This alone halves the work.
2. **Build an asset library once, reuse forever.** Sprite sheets for Wick (idle, walk, talk, scared, casting light), each party member, each monster, and one background per location (day and night versions made by color tint, not redrawn).
3. **Scene budget.** Each scene runs 30 to 45 seconds. Only 1 or 2 scenes per episode may be "action" scenes. The rest are dialogue or narration over a still or lightly looping image.
4. **Let the narrator carry the story.** Strong voice over plus simple visuals costs far less than complex animation.
5. **Cheap motion tricks (pixel style makes these look great):** camera pans, slow zooms, screen shake, flickering light, drifting fog, parallax layers, silhouettes, text boxes, fade to black, color flashes.
6. **Fights use impact frames.** Show the wind up, a bright flash, then the result. No full fight choreography.
7. **Use a low frame rate.** 8 to 12 frames per second is on style for pixel art and cheaper.
8. **Fixed templates reused every episode:** a 5 second title card, the "Last time" recap, and the A or B choice screen.
9. **Recap with old footage.** The 10 second recap reuses stills or clips from the previous episode instead of new animation.
10. **One new thing per episode.** Either one new character or one new location, never both.
11. **Approve the script before animating.** Never animate a scene that has not been read and approved. Fix a single scene instead of redoing a whole episode.
12. **Keep a bank of 3 episodes** made ahead, so a heavy day never breaks the daily schedule.

## 4.3 Standard 4 minute episode template
| Part | Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Title card + recap | 0:00 to 0:25 | Reused template and old stills |
